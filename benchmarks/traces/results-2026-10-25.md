# Repeated work in System1-Agents traces, 2026-10-25

Recorded on one NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada with [`record_s1a.py`](record_s1a.py) from
System1-Agents `9b20b98`. Open-Jev-9B decided: the native worker with #179's change, no
prefix switch, the merged export's default 4,096-token limit. Fronts that need a chat
model used Qwen3-8B through vLLM 0.32 with thinking off. Flights ran against live Google
Flights (`hl=en&gl=CH`, one route per episode); the form fixtures are
`evals.recovery.live`'s three tasks with recovery off and on. Each Flights and form
episode was one invocation with its own `--group`, and the invocations were concatenated
into one trace per front; each keeps its own clock, so replay these with `--sequential`. `zurich-london` is System1-Agents' own recording of a Jev 1.13
run (`docs/results/laya-browser-shaping/zurich-london.jsonl`), converted with
`tracefile.py from-ticks`.

Only the [samples](samples/) are in this repository; the other traces copy third-party
pages or prompt text and stay outside it. The ticket-router row can be recomputed from
its sample.

## Shares of prefill tokens that could be skipped

| Trace | Episodes / measured decisions (unserved) | Open-Jev: within a request | + server cache, last 8 | Cua-S1: within a request | + server cache, last 8 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Google Flights, live | 28 / 93 (5) | 89.2% | 90.8% | 4.4% | 31.8% |
| `zurich-london` | 1 / 10 (2); Cua-S1 9 (3) | 91.5% | 91.9% | 4.7% | 35.6% |
| Form fixtures | 36 / 108 | 60.5% | 61.6% | 9.5% | 49.4% |
| 2048 | 5 / 800 | 58.2% | 59.7% | 0% | 46.6% |
| Ticket router | 1 / 30 | 59.8% | 59.8% | 0% | 45.3% |
| Blackjack | 100 / 119 | 41.8% | 41.8% | 0% | 77.2% |

Each layout is measured on its own tokens. Unserved requests are those recorded as
failed (the five Flights results pages over 4,096 tokens) or that the worker would refuse
(two `zurich-london` pages over Open-Jev-9B's limit; three with more than 26 click
targets for Cua-S1). The Cua-S1 "within a request" share is what Decider's planner would
skip; the Cua-S1 worker runs none.

- No state came back exactly in any trace.
- Where the state comes first (Open-Jev), sharing within a request is nearly all of the
  reuse; a cache of recent prompts adds at most 1.6 points.
- Where each question's goal comes first (Cua-S1), one sequence per question leaves
  little to share within a request, but the system prompt and the goal repeat at every
  step, so a cache of recent prompts could skip 32 to 77%. Blackjack's 77% needs a
  server-side cache that ignores episode boundaries: each hand is its own short episode
  (84 of 100 take one decision); within episodes it is 12%.

## Pace

| Trace | Think time p50 / p90 | Decision latency p50 / p90 (recorded) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Google Flights | 0.67 / 6.7 s | 6.4 / 7.7 s |
| Form fixtures | 1.2 / 3.4 s | 1.07 / 1.27 s |
| 2048 | 0.67 / 0.68 s | 0.14 / 0.15 s |
| Ticket router, blackjack (no chat model) | 1 to 3 ms | 0.08 to 0.23 s |

Agents send one request at a time; without a chat model the next request follows the
last response within milliseconds.

## Caveats

- Trajectories depend on the deciding model. Open-Jev-9B ends most Flights episodes at
  the second decision (22 of 28), as System1-Agents' A40 runs also show, so Flights
  episodes are short.
- Flights results pages can exceed the 9B export's 4,096-token default; the export can be
  made with a longer `--max-length`.
- One GPU, one decision model, one chat model, one day.
